Diagnosing Verify Amnesia

Why your agent isn't failing because it's dumb, but because it's opaque.

A startup founder messaged a framework maintainer six weeks post-acquisition. The OpenAI bill for one weekend was $4,200 [3].

The logs revealed a monolithic agent loop attempting to sync order data to a CRM. The sequence was mechanically perfect, and entirely catastrophic:

Plan
Tool
Retrieve
Execute
Verify
Output

1. PLANNING: The agent set the goal to sync data.

> LEDGER REVEAL: Iteration #4,800 | Cumulative Cost: $4,200 | Recursion Depth: MAX

This loop repeated 4,800 times per hour for 63 hours [3]. The failure did not originate at the tool call—the API did exactly what it was supposed to do under load. The failure originated at the Verify stage, which suffered from Verify Amnesia, evaluating the 4,800th attempt in a vacuum and passing it forward because the immediate step looked mechanically sound.

"We sell decomposition as a way to make agents smarter. That is the wrong argument. The real moat is legibility."

The Legibility Tax

When production teams document Agentic RAG failure modes, they frequently encounter "Retrieval Thrash" [38]. An agent repeatedly reformulates queries without converging, burning through budgets while the internal verifier simply flags the answer as "incomplete" [38]. LangGraph's own official tutorial required a hard rewrite_count cap to stop infinite loops [38].

The counterargument is that a sufficiently large context window and a better system prompt can teach a monolith to track its own budget. But research into context bloat proves that as tool outputs accumulate, the model's attention to its own core instructions degrades, sometimes falling below closed-book performance [38]. You cannot prompt your way out of an architectural deficit.

BEFORE

You decompose agents to make them "smarter."

TURN

The $4,200 weekend proves monoliths aren't dumber; they are just opaque.

AFTER

Decomposition is a legibility tax that makes infinite loops mechanically impossible.

BEFORE

You trust the Verify stage to catch bad tool outputs.

TURN

The Verify stage has amnesia; it passes a 429 error on iteration #4,800 because it only looks at the current step.

AFTER

You must externalize the ledger into an orchestration layer that tracks cumulative state across the loop.

Triage Checklist

  • Plan Define a hard recursion depth and wall-clock timebox before the loop starts.
  • Tool Audit every external boundary to ensure idempotency, making safe retries possible.
  • Verify Externalize the ledger. Build a supervisor layer outside the worker's context that tracks cumulative cost and iteration count.
  • Output Force a "best-effort with uncertainty" fallback when any budget tripwire fires, rather than allowing a silent refusal.

"Stop falling for Verify Amnesia. The tool call didn't break your budget. The verifier's lack of a cumulative ledger did."

Curing the Loop

In the next 24 hours, pick your most expensive production agent and wrap its main execution loop in a stateful decorator that hard-kills the process if cumulative USD spend exceeds $50 or recursion depth exceeds 8.
