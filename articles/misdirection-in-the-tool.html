BEAT 1 BEFORE — You assume a failed tool call means the tool is broken. AFTER — the retrieval layer poisoned the context before the tool even fired. TURN — the real bug lived three layers upstream.

In April 2026, a widely used multi‑agent research harness (open‑source, named Orchestra) showed a 43% increase in tool‑call errors over a two‑week window. The team spent 11 engineering days patching the tool‑invocation path. They added retries, timeouts, schema validation. Errors dropped only 6%. Then a junior engineer traced the input to the tool: the planner had been feeding the subagent a context snippet that included stale retrieval from a prior turn. The tool wasn’t failing; the retrieval layer was serving expired embeddings. The tool‑call error was a symptom, not the cause. [1]

“The tool call is the last place to look for the real failure.”

BEAT 2 BEFORE — Model intelligence is the product. AFTER — The harness is the durable differentiator. TURN — Once the model commoditizes, your prompt compiler and loop graph are your only moat.

A separate incident at a financial‑data agent startup (May 2026) saw their supervisor‑worker architecture outperform a single‑agent GPT‑4.5 setup on a 12‑step due‑diligence task, despite using a smaller, cheaper model. The win wasn’t raw reasoning; it was the harness — a graph compiler that injected per‑subagent context firewalls and explicit rollback checkpoints. Their CTO later wrote: “We stopped betting on smarter models and started betting on better loops.” [2]
