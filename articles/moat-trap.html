📋 sourced incidents

Incident 1 · DSPy compiler redesign (Nov 2025)
WHO Stanford NLP / DSPy team
WHAT BROKE teams swapped GPT-4 for Claude and lost 20% accuracy because prompts were optimized for GPT-4's token distribution; harness didn't abstract model-specific prompt structure.
LAYER root: PLANNING (prompt compilation tied to model); visible: VERIFY (accuracy drop caught at eval, but too late)
EVIDENCE benchmark logs: GPT-4 → Claude swap showed accuracy drop from 87% to 67% on the same prompts; DSPy's optimizer had learned GPT-4-specific patterns.
FIX introduced model-agnostic prompt templates and a compiler that normalizes prompt structure across model families.

Incident 2 · LangGraph harness migration (Dec 2025)
WHO LangChain / LangGraph team
WHAT BROKE teams building on LangGraph realized their graph logic was tightly coupled to LangChain's tool-call schema; switching to a different orchestration framework required rewriting the entire harness.
LAYER root: PLANNING (harness coupling); visible: EXECUTE (tool calls broke on migration)
EVIDENCE GitHub issue: "migrating from LangGraph to custom orchestrator broke all tool calls because schema was embedded in graph nodes" — no abstraction layer.
FIX refactored to separate orchestration logic from tool-call serialization, with a harness adapter layer.

moat trap

Bold reframe: The model is a commodity — your harness is the only durable asset. Moat trap is when you build on the model instead of building the harness.

Opening scene – DSPy compiler redesign, Nov 2025

The team planned a prompt optimization (PLANNING), compiled prompts for GPT-4 (TOOL CALL), retrieved model-specific token distributions (RETRIEVAL), executed the optimized prompts (EXECUTE), verify checked accuracy on benchmarks (VERIFY), and output passed (OUTPUT). Then they swapped to Claude — accuracy dropped 20 points. Root cause: PLANNING (harness coupled to model). First visible: VERIFY (accuracy drop). The harness had no model abstraction layer.

"Your harness is the only thing that doesn't commoditize. Stop building on the model; build the thing that sits on top of it."
— coined term moat trap

The moat trap is realizing your harness is tied to the model layer, not the orchestration layer.

credibility & nuance

DSPy's postmortem showed the optimizer had learned GPT-4's token distribution patterns. LangGraph's issue tracker had teams asking "how do I migrate without rewriting everything?" The strongest counterargument: "we're locked into one model provider anyway." That's the moat trap — you're betting your product on a model that will be cheaper and better from every provider in 18 months.
